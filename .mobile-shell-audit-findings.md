# DOONO Mobile Header and Long-Page Navigation Audit

## Confirmed problems from the supplied phone screenshot

The authenticated header was over-compressed on a narrow viewport. The notification bell sat directly against the truncated brand, the role badge displayed an ellipsis without enough context, and the logout/avatar controls competed for the remaining horizontal space. The fixed footer and guide controls occupied the bottom edge while the last dashboard card continued behind them, making it unclear whether more content existed and leaving no direct way to continue downward or return to the top.

## Repairs implemented

The mobile Navbar now uses a compact role-aware arrangement. Its menu button exposes the current open state to assistive technology and targets the actual Sidebar element. The header brand remains clickable and the context line is hidden on very narrow screens to protect the action controls. Logout shortens to `Exit` at the smallest breakpoint, while the role badge and avatar retain touch-safe dimensions. The notification bell remains visible and uses a scalable SVG icon rather than an emoji.

A shared `ScrollNavigator` is now mounted in `DashboardLayout`, so every authenticated long page receives a reading-progress bar plus contextual `Continue` and `Back to top` controls. The navigator is placed on the left side of the viewport while the Guide and Role tour controls remain on the right, preventing overlap. It appears only when the document is actually long enough to scroll.

Footer-safe spacing was retained and strengthened for authenticated content, including mobile safe-area padding. The scroll controls sit above the footer and do not cover the guide launcher or the last content card.

## Validation evidence

The frontend production build passed successfully after the final changes, and `git diff --check` passed. Shell integration checks confirm `ScrollNavigator`, synchronized `menuOpen` state, the `dono-sidebar` accessibility target, and sidebar-aware footer behavior. The Vite bundle-size warning remains non-blocking. Visual confirmation on the user’s actual phone is still recommended after deployment because browser viewport chrome and device safe areas vary.
